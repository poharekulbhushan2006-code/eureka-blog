### 2. Superposition & Schrödinger's Cat

In everyday life, a book is either open or closed; a cat is either asleep or awake. Classical physics assumes definite states.

In quantum mechanics, a system is described by a state vector $|\Psi\rangle$ existing as a linear combination of eigenstates:

```
|Ψ⟩ = α|State A⟩ + β|State B⟩
```

Until a measurement occurs, the system does not have a single classical value; it has a probability distribution whose amplitudes evolve deterministically according to the **Schrödinger Equation** (`iħ ∂Ψ/∂t = ĤΨ`).

> **Hogwarts counterpart:** Imagine the moving staircases of Hogwarts. Before an observer interacts with them, the staircase's position is not physically fixed—it exists across a spectrum of possible coordinates.

### 3. Entanglement — Non-Local Quantum Correlation

Albert Einstein famously criticized quantum entanglement as *"spooky action at a distance"* (*spukhafte Fernwirkung*). If two particles are generated in an entangled state, measuring the quantum spin of one particle instantaneously determines the outcome of measuring the other, even if separated by billions of kilometers.

<figure class="quantum-card-figure single-card">
  <img src="/images/quantum/5-entanglement.jpg" alt="5. Entanglement — Linked across distances" loading="lazy">
</figure>

In 2022, the **Nobel Prize in Physics** was awarded to Alain Aspect, John Clauser, and Anton Zeilinger for experimentally confirming that local realism is violated—nature is fundamentally non-local in its correlations.

> **Crucial scientific constraint:** Entanglement cannot be used to transmit messages faster than light (no-signaling theorem), because each measurement yields a purely random outcome until compared over a classical communication channel.

### 4. The Uncertainty Principle & Wave-Particle Interference

Formulated by Werner Heisenberg in 1927, the **Uncertainty Principle** states that complementary physical properties cannot be simultaneously measured with arbitrary precision:

$$\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \ge \frac{\hbar}{2}$$

This is not a limitation of our instruments—it is a fundamental property of wave mechanics. A localized particle requires a broad spectrum of wave frequencies, creating intrinsic momentum spread.

### 7. Quantum Teleportation: What It Actually Does

In fiction, teleportation moves human flesh across space. In quantum mechanics, **quantum teleportation** transfers the complete information state of a quantum particle to another distant particle without moving the physical particle itself.

### ⏳ Timeline: Major Milestones in Quantum Science

* **1900 — Max Planck's Quantum Hypothesis:** Planck introduces the constant $h$ to resolve the ultraviolet catastrophe in black-body radiation, revealing that energy exchange occurs in discrete packets (quanta).
* **1905 — Albert Einstein & The Photoelectric Effect:** Einstein demonstrates that light consists of localized wave-packets (photons), earning the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics.
* **1913 — Niels Bohr's Atomic Model:** Bohr models the hydrogen atom with quantized electron orbits, explaining atomic spectral lines.
* **1925–1926 — Matrix & Wave Mechanics:** Werner Heisenberg discovers matrix mechanics; Erwin Schrödinger formulates his continuous wave equation.
* **1927 — The Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle:** Heisenberg proves that complementary physical quantities (position and momentum) cannot be known simultaneously with arbitrary precision: `Δx · Δp ≥ ℏ/2`.
* **1935 — The EPR Paradox & Schrödinger's Cat:** Einstein, Podolsky, and Rosen question quantum completeness; Schrödinger devises his famous thought experiment.
* **1964 — Bell's Theorem:** John Stewart Bell publishes the mathematical proof that no local hidden-variable theory can reproduce the predictions of quantum mechanics.
* **1982 — Alain Aspect's Landmark Experiment:** Aspect, Dalibard, and Roger deliver the first conclusive experimental test showing that nature violates Bell's inequalities.
* **2022 — Nobel Prize in Physics:** Awarded to Alain Aspect, John Clauser, and Anton Zeilinger for groundbreaking experiments with entangled photons and pioneering quantum information science.
